Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Spore-informed agroclimatic modelling improves the timing of grapevine downy mildew management in subtropical vineyards" for consideration as an original research article in the European Journal of Agronomy.

The manuscript addresses a practical agronomic question that is central to crop protection in humid subtropical viticulture: how field monitoring and agroclimatic modelling can be combined to improve the timing of grapevine downy mildew protection. This question is particularly relevant where conventional calendar-based spray programmes remain common, but the biological basis of first seasonal infection may differ from the oospore-driven primary infection systems for which many mechanistic warning models were originally developed.

The main contribution of the study is a field-tested decision-support framework rather than a stand-alone modelling exercise. We developed a spore-informed extension of the First Seasonal Infection Model (FSIM), hereafter FSIM-S, that incorporates field-observed airborne sporangia as a dynamic spore-pressure constraint. This formulation links on-farm inoculum monitoring, meteorological drivers, and process-based disease modelling to management-relevant timing information.

The work is supported by three years of observations from two commercial vineyards and one experimental orchard in Guangxi, China, including phenology, airborne sporangia, symptom onset, management records, and site-specific meteorological data. Across validation datasets, incorporating spore pressure reduced the mean absolute error of first-infection timing from 9.3 to 6.3 days and the root mean square error from 10.6 to 6.5 days. Regional scenario analysis for 2000-2024 further identified a stable high-risk window around DOY 180-210 and suggested that risk-based timing could avoid 2-5 early preventive fungicide applications per season compared with a conventional calendar-based programme. We interpret this as improved timing of protection, not as a replacement for disease management during high-risk periods.

We believe the manuscript fits the scope of the European Journal of Agronomy by combining field-based horticultural experimentation with agroclimatic modelling to address a practical crop-production and management problem. The study also aligns with the journal's emphasis on Smart Agriculture/Precision Farming, as it integrates on-farm monitoring, site-specific meteorological data, and model-guided decision support to improve the temporal precision of crop protection. Its central emphasis is the agronomic translation of field observations into actionable disease-management timing for subtropical vineyard systems.
